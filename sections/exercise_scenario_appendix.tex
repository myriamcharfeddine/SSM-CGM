\section{Exercise Scenario Supplement}
\label{app:exercise_scenario}

\subsection{Supplementary Detector-Informed Exercise Modeling Results}
\label{subsec:exercise_scenario_supplement}

This appendix contains the existing detailed result figures supporting
Section~\ref{subsec:detector_scenario_modeling}. Each figure uses the fixed
held-out participant evaluation or its documented validation counterpart; no
figure was regenerated for the report.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{exercise_scenario/figure_10_robustness_subsets.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Robustness across detector episode subsets.}
    Predictive gain and planned terminal effects are shown for all primary,
    high-confidence, and strict-recovery episode definitions.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_robustness_supp}
\end{figure}
Figure~\ref{fig:exercise_robustness_supp} shows positive factual-future gain in
all three subsets. The intervals overlap, so the restricted subsets support
robustness but do not establish that one detector subset is superior.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{exercise_scenario/figure_11_common_anchor_mae_and_bias.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Exercise-anchor forecasting performance: original versus
    detector-trained stream.}
    Forecast-only MAE and bias are compared on identical exercise anchors.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_anchor_comparison_supp}
\end{figure}
On the common exercise-anchor cohort, the detector-trained forecast-only model
improves MAE by approximately 0.69~mg/dL and reduces the canonical positive
bias. This cohort-specific preservation result is not a full-stream metric.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{exercise_scenario/figure_12_full_cohort_mae_and_bias.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Full validation-cohort performance: original versus
    detector-trained stream.}
    Validation-stream MAE and prediction-minus-observation bias are reported
    for the canonical and selected detector-informed checkpoints.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_validation_stream_supp}
\end{figure}
The validation stream anticipates the final test pattern: absolute error changes
little, while the detector-informed checkpoint shifts downward. The final
generalization claim is based on the test-stream analysis in
Figure~\ref{fig:exercise_full_stream}, not this validation diagnostic.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.94\linewidth]{exercise_scenario/appendix_figure_2_factual_future_gain_paired_lines.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Factual future exercise improves held-out forecasting.}
    Participant-level paired changes in pinball loss, MAE, and 60-minute MAE
    compare unknown future with the revealed factual exercise path.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_factual_gain_supp}
\end{figure}
The paired view in Figure~\ref{fig:exercise_factual_gain_supp} complements the
cohort averages in Table~\ref{tab:exercise_factual_future}. It shows that the
reported gain is a predictive comparison on identical anchors and preserves
participant clustering.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{exercise_scenario/figure_4_canonical_vs_trained_pathway.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Canonical versus trained exercise pathway on identical anchors.}
    Planned light, moderate, and vigorous responses are compared with planned
    no exercise for both checkpoints.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_canonical_trained_supp}
\end{figure}
The canonical pathway is practically flat, while the detector-informed pathway
is nonzero and differentiated. This is evidence of pathway activation and
model sensitivity, not validation against a physiological target.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.90\linewidth]{exercise_scenario/figure_3_exercise_counterfactual_trajectories.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Detector-informed planned exercise trajectories.}
    Mean forecast changes relative to planned no exercise are shown over the
    one-hour decoder horizon with participant-bootstrap intervals.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_trajectories_supp}
\end{figure}
The learned responses increase gradually in magnitude and preserve the light,
moderate, and vigorous ordering. Their horizon-local form should be interpreted
with the duration and timing limitation in
Figure~\ref{fig:exercise_temporal_sweeps}.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.88\linewidth]{exercise_scenario/figure_6_effect_by_starting_glucose.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Model-implied exercise effect by starting glucose.}
    Planned terminal responses are stratified below 105~mg/dL, from 105 to
    180~mg/dL, and above 180~mg/dL.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_starting_glucose_supp}
\end{figure}
Starting glucose is the clearest interpretable gradient in the learned
responses. Larger modeled reductions at higher glucose indicate contextual
conditioning, but may also reflect the residual-current target and the
observational state distribution.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth]{exercise_scenario/figure_5_participant_effect_distribution.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Participant-level terminal-effect distributions.}
    Each distribution summarizes participant-mean planned 60-minute responses
    for one strain profile.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_participant_effects_supp}
\end{figure}
The participant summaries show heterogeneous magnitude even though nearly all
planned-profile effects are negative in the selected checkpoint. They describe
model behavior across participants rather than individual physiological
sensitivity.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{exercise_scenario/figure_9_exercise_component_ablation.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Exercise component ablations on identical held-out anchors.}
    Factual-future gain, factual MAE, mean absolute scenario effect, and
    terminal effect consistency are compared across coherent edits.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_component_ablation_supp}
\end{figure}
The component ablations show redundancy among exercise variables. Labels-only
retain more gain than HR and steps alone, but no single variable accounts for
the combined pathway benefit, and mask changes remain part of each ablation.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.94\linewidth]{exercise_scenario/figure_i2_permutation_importance.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Participant-level grouped permutation importance.}
    Loss of predictive gain and increase in factual MAE are summarized across
    five permutation seeds.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_permutation_supp}
\end{figure}
Exact strain identity has modest importance and uncertainty across seeds is
large; several individual inputs have effects close to zero. The result is
consistent with reliance on a redundant block and does not assign causal
feature effects.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.94\linewidth]{exercise_scenario/figure_i3_scenario_effect_probes.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Linear probes of the scenario-effect representation.}
    Held-out classification and regression probes compare the
    detector-informed effect branch with the shared canonical branch.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_effect_probes_supp}
\end{figure}
Starting-glucose state is moderately recoverable, while strain and cadence are
weakly recoverable. Negative held-out $R^2$ for raw HR, steps, or duration means
that a simple linear map is unreliable; it does not show that the nonlinear
decoder ignores the variable.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.94\linewidth]{exercise_scenario/figure_i4_hidden_state_probes.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Secondary probes of the historical hidden state.}
    Linear probe performance is compared between canonical and
    detector-informed historical representations.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_hidden_probes_supp}
\end{figure}
Current glucose remains strongly represented, and the three recent-exercise
indicators become linearly recoverable after training. Their recoverability
does not imply predictive necessity, as zeroing recent history leaves global
test behavior nearly unchanged.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth,height=0.70\textheight,keepaspectratio]{exercise_scenario/figure_i8_subgroup_heterogeneity.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Supported subgroup heterogeneity.}
    Participant-mean terminal effects are shown only for cells meeting the
    prespecified participant and anchor support thresholds.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_subgroups_supp}
\end{figure}
Strain ordering is broadly stable and starting glucose is the clearest
interpretable gradient. Site and clinical-group differences remain exploratory
because subgroup composition, observed state, and sensor support can differ.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{exercise_scenario/figure_i10_scenario_latent_pca.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{PCA of the detector-informed scenario-effect representation.}
    The first two standardized principal components are colored by strain and
    starting-glucose stratum.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_pca_supp}
\end{figure}
The first two components explain only 20.7\% and 8.4\% of latent variance, and
no clear class clustering is visible. PCA is descriptive and does not establish
mechanistic separation.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[height=0.76\textheight,keepaspectratio]{exercise_scenario/figure_7_individual_examples.pdf}
  \caption{%
    \textbf{Reproducibly selected held-out exercise examples.}
    Fixed selection rules illustrate an in-range anchor, a high-glucose anchor,
    a low-glucose anchor, and a case with large factual forecast improvement.%
  }
  \label{fig:exercise_examples_supp}
\end{figure}
The examples illustrate pathway behavior but do not replace cohort-level
evaluation. Cases in which all forecasts miss the observed trajectory are
evidence that important drivers remain unobserved, not evidence against the
paired cohort result.
